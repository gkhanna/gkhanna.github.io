Profile

Physics at the edge of computation

Gaurav Khanna is a black hole physicist and computational scientist whose research studies gravitational radiation from compact objects, the late-time behavior of fields in curved spacetime, and the numerical infrastructure required for difficult simulations. His work spans black hole perturbation theory, extreme and intermediate mass-ratio inspirals, quasinormal modes, black hole ringdown, cosmic censorship, and quantum gravity.

At URI, he also leads research computing strategy and the Institute for AI & Computational Research, helping faculty, students, and regional partners use high-performance computing, data science, artificial intelligence, and emerging computational tools in research and education.

Research

Black holes, gravitational waves, and the structure of spacetime

Khanna's research uses analytical insight and numerical computation to study systems where gravity is strong, spacetime is dynamic, and precision modeling matters for gravitational-wave astronomy.

01

Black hole perturbation theory

Modeling the dynamics of small bodies moving through black hole spacetimes, including time-domain Teukolsky calculations and waveform generation for extreme and small mass-ratio binaries.

02

Gravitational waves and ringdown

Understanding inspiral, plunge, merger, quasinormal modes, and late-time tails in the gravitational radiation produced by black hole binaries.

03

Extremal black holes and gravitational hair

Studying whether special black holes can carry measurable structure beyond mass, spin, and charge, and how such signatures might appear to distant observers.

04

Scientific and high-performance computing

Developing practical computational methods and resourceful hardware strategies, from GPU and cluster computing to the well-known PlayStation 3 Gravity Grid.

Leadership

Institute for AI & Computational Research

The Institute for AI & Computational Research at URI brings together artificial intelligence, data science, high-performance computing, and quantum computing. It grew from URI's Center for Computational Research and is designed to support faculty research, student training, infrastructure, seminars, consulting, workshops, seed programs, and regional collaboration.

As founding director, Khanna helps connect research computing infrastructure with the human support needed for adoption: consultations, training, interdisciplinary partnerships, and the translation of advanced computation into research outcomes.

Computing

Resourceful supercomputing

The PlayStation 3 Gravity Grid became a widely covered example of using commodity gaming hardware for serious scientific computation. The project exploited the Cell processor and MPI parallelization to support black hole simulations, high-precision calculations, and benchmark studies.

The broader idea remains current: science advances when researchers can combine mathematical creativity, open tools, careful software, and affordable computational infrastructure.
